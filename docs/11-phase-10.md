# 11 — Phase 10: remote, video and surround

From [the plan, §12](01-implementation-plan.md#phase-10-remote-video-surround-10-weeks), §6.5 (LAN and remote
clients) and §11 (Logic Remote, Surround and Spatial Audio, Video and sync). Spatial audio follows
[ADR-0012](adr/0012-spatial-audio.md); video and listen-in follow [ADR-0013](adr/0013-video-and-listen-in.md).

**Exit criterion.** A tablet runs the session from the live room and hears it; a movie plays locked to the transport
and gets its soundtrack back; and a project mixes in surround and immersive formats, monitors binaurally, and exports
ADM BWF that an independent renderer agrees with. The Playwright golden path covers it on the null device:
1. **Remote.**
   - Find the studio by DNS-SD (`_anode._tcp`), pair a second browser context (a phone/tablet viewport) and open the
     `remote` route.
   - Play and stop from the remote; move a channel's fader: the desktop shows it.
   - Launch a Live Loops cell from the remote's grid.
   - Record from the remote's drum pads on an armed Drum Machine Designer track: the region holds the hits.
2. **Listen-in.** The remote turns listen-in on: it receives the master as Opus, decodes it, and a 1 kHz test tone
   playing in the project is found in the decoded audio (its frequency within 2 Hz, level within 1 dB), less than
   500 ms behind the transport.
3. **Video.** Import a test movie (generated by FFmpeg: a frame counter and timecode burn-in, a beep every second,
   scene cuts at known frames):
   - The movie window shows the frame of the playhead: playing and after locates, within one frame.
   - The movie track shows thumbnails; Detect Scene Cuts adds markers at the known cuts, within one frame.
   - Import Audio from Movie: the beeps land on their frames, within one millisecond.
   - With a movie start offset (the movie's first frame at bar 2), the same holds.
   - Export Audio to Movie: the new movie's audio (read back by FFprobe and FFmpeg) is the project's bounce, aligned
     to its frames within one millisecond, and its video is unchanged (stream copy).
4. **Surround.** Set the project to 7.1.4:
   - The Surround Panner on a mono track, puck at left surround: a multichannel bounce has the track's energy in Ls,
     and at least 20 dB less in the other speakers; the LFE send puts its low-passed copy in LFE.
   - An object track (3D Object Panner) automated from front left to rear right overhead: its energy moves from L to
     the top rear right speaker over the automation.
   - A stereo effect on a 7.1.4 bus processes every channel; the down-mix to stereo monitoring matches ITU-R BS.775
     coefficients within 0.1 dB.
5. **Binaural.** With binaural monitoring on, a source panned to 90° left reaches the left ear at least 6 dB louder
   than the right, and the right ear 0.5–0.8 ms later.
6. **ADM BWF.** Export the 7.1.4 mix as ADM BWF (bed plus objects):
   - The file carries `chna` and `axml` chunks valid per ITU-R BS.2076 (checked by the EBU ADM Renderer, `ear`).
   - `ear` renders it to 7.1.4: each speaker's signal matches Anode's own render within 1 dB of RMS over each second.
7. Save, close and reopen: everything is intact.

The contracts below are committed code. Each workstream builds against them and changes them only with a note in its
report. A REST change means regenerating `engine/src/Anode.Server/openapi.json`
(`ANODE_UPDATE_OPENAPI=1 dotnet test`) and the editor client (`nx run api-client:generate`).

## Scope

**In:**
- **Remote (`remote` route):** the Logic Remote equivalent for touch, in the same Angular build served on the LAN:
  - transport and LCD; a mixer (faders, pan, mute, solo, arm, sends) with banks; Smart Controls of the selected track;
    the Live Loops grid and scenes; key-command pads (configurable); a touch keyboard (scales, octave, velocity by
    touch position) and drum pads (a DMD kit's pads, or 16 by the GM map), sending MIDI as live MIDI; the Step
    Sequencer of the selected pattern region; the track list to select.
  - Layout for phones (portrait) and tablets (landscape), large touch targets, no hover.
  - **Discovery:** the server advertises `_anode._tcp` over DNS-SD (macOS through the Phase 8 `DNSServiceRegister`
    path) with the project name and the pairing port; pairing as today (token, QR code).
- **Listen-in:** the master (stereo, or the stereo down-mix of a surround master) encoded as Opus, sent to subscribed
  remotes over a WebSocket, decoded in the browser; latency shown; at most 4 listeners.
- **Video:**
  - A movie per project: import (MOV, MP4, M4V; H.264/HEVC/ProRes as the platform plays them), a start position (the
    movie's first frame at a project position) and an offset; the movie's frame rate and timecode start.
  - The movie window (floating or in the inspector) and the movie track with thumbnails, both locked to the transport:
    playing, scrubbing, locating, cycling, varispeed.
  - Scene markers: Detect Scene Cuts adds markers (a marker kind), editable.
  - Import Audio from Movie (as a new track at the movie's position), Export Audio to Movie (replace or add the audio
    track, the video stream copied), Remove Movie.
  - Remotes on the LAN play the movie too (served with HTTP range requests).
- **Surround and immersive:**
  - **Formats:** mono, stereo, LCR, quad, 5.0, 5.1, 7.0, 7.1, 7.1.2, 7.1.4, 9.1.6, and Ambisonics out of scope (below).
    The project has a surround format; the master and buses have a channel format; a track's channel strip has an
    input format and an output format (the bus it feeds).
  - **Surround Panner** (2D): angle, diversity, centre level, LFE level, elevation for formats with height; per input
    channel for stereo inputs (spread).
  - **3D Object Panner** (objects): azimuth, elevation, distance, size; a track is a bed channel or an object (up to
    118 objects, as the ADM profile for 7.1.4 beds allows).
  - **Rendering:** beds pass through; objects render to the speaker layout (VBAP over the layout's triangulation, with
    size spread); the down-mixer to smaller layouts and to stereo (ITU-R BS.775); multichannel metering (per speaker,
    and loudness).
  - **Effects:** linear effects (gain, EQ, filters, delays, the convolution reverb) run per channel; dynamics link
    across channels; stereo-only effects on a multichannel bus run on L/R only, with a note in the UI.
  - **Binaural monitoring:** the speaker feed (or the objects directly) rendered through HRTFs (SADIE II, SOFA) for
    headphones; head-tracking is out of scope.
  - **Device output:** the master's channels map to the device's outputs (a speaker map), or binaural/down-mix to the
    first pair.
  - **ADM BWF export** (ITU-R BS.2076, BS.2088 for >4 GB, the EBU ADM production profile for beds and objects): `chna`
    and `axml`, objects with position automation as `audioBlockFormat`s; also re-import of an ADM BWF into tracks
    (bed and objects) for re-render.

**Out:**
- Ambisonics (formats, panners and decoding), head tracking, personalised HRTFs.
- Dolby's renderer, SDKs, trademarks or file formats beyond ADM BWF (ADR-0008).
- WebRTC for listen-in (a WebSocket is enough on a LAN; ADR-0013).
- Video editing (cutting the movie), video effects, multiple movies.
- Remote control of plug-in editors.

### Rules the contracts imply

- **Formats and channels:** a speaker layout is an ordered list of ADM speaker labels (`M+030`, `M-030`, `M+000`,
  `LFE1`, `M+110`, …) with nominal positions; the engine's buffers carry a strip's channels in that order. Stereo is
  the two-speaker layout `M+030`/`M-030`; existing projects are stereo throughout and play as before.
- **Panning:** a strip's panner maps its input channels to its output format: stereo pan/balance when the output is
  stereo (as today), the Surround Panner for a surround output, or an object (the strip's output becomes an object of
  the master renderer, not a bed channel). Panner parameters are automation addresses (`surround:angle`,
  `surround:diversity`, `surround:lfe`, `surround:elevation`, `object:azimuth`, `object:elevation`,
  `object:distance`, `object:size`) and Smart Control targets.
- **Rendering:** beds sum into the master; objects render to the master's layout at the master, after the bed sum
  and before the master's inserts; monitoring (down-mix or binaural) happens after the master and before the device,
  and never changes bounces unless the bounce asks for the monitoring render.
- **Bounce:** a surround master bounces to one interleaved multichannel WAV (WAVE_FORMAT_EXTENSIBLE, channel mask) or
  to one file per speaker; ADM BWF is a separate export.
- **ADM:** beds are `DirectSpeakers` channels, objects `Objects` with one `audioBlockFormat` per automation change
  (with interpolation) or per block of 20 ms at most; positions are polar (azimuth, elevation, distance) as the
  object panner's.
- **Video:** the project tick ↔ movie time mapping is the movie's start position and offset through the tempo map;
  the server holds the movie metadata; the editor plays the file, seeking to the transport at the frame boundary and
  correcting drift when playing; FFmpeg does the media work as a separate process (ADR-0013).
- **Listen-in:** the host writes the stereo master (or its down-mix) into a ring the server reads; the server encodes
  Opus at 48 kHz, 20 ms frames, 128 kbit/s and sends each frame with its transport sample position, so the remote can
  show latency.
- **Remote MIDI:** the touch keyboard and pads send live MIDI through the existing `/midi/live` path, so recording,
  step input and MIDI FX behave as with a keyboard.

### Contract notes

Where the committed code differs from, or adds to, the shapes planned for this commit, and the shapes chosen where
the plan left latitude:
- **Formats** (`Spatial.cs`): `ChannelFormat` (`mono | stereo | lcr | quad | surround50 | surround51 | surround70 |
  surround71 | surround712 | surround714 | surround916`). `SpeakerLabels` (constants: `Left` `M+030`, `Right`
  `M-030`, `Centre` `M+000`, `Lfe` `LFE1`, `LeftSurround`/`RightSurround` `M±110`, `LeftSide`/`RightSide` `M±090`,
  `LeftRear`/`RightRear` `M±135`, `LeftWide`/`RightWide` `M±060`, `LeftFrontQuad`/`RightFrontQuad` `M±045`,
  `TopFrontLeft`/`Right` `U±045`, `TopMiddleLeft`/`Right` `U±090`, `TopRearLeft`/`Right` `U±135`).
  `Speaker(label, azimuth, elevation, lfe = false)` and `SpeakerLayout(format, name, ituName?, speakers)` (with
  `ChannelCount`, `HasHeight`, `HasLfe`, `IndexOf(label)`, not serialised); `SpeakerLayouts.All`, `Of(format)`,
  `ChannelCount(format)`, `IsSurround(format)` (wider than stereo). Degrees in the ADM convention: azimuth 0 ahead,
  **positive to the left**, −180…180; elevation positive up. **Channel order is the ADM/SMPTE order**: L, R, C, LFE,
  then sides before rears, wides, the upper layer front to back (7.1.4: `M+030 M-030 M+000 LFE1 M+090 M-090 M+135
  M-135 U+045 U-045 U+135 U-135`); a WAVE_FORMAT_EXTENSIBLE file orders by its channel mask (rears before sides in
  7.x), so A's writer and reader reorder. Positions: BS.2051 where it defines the system (stereo 0+2+0, 5.1 0+5+0, 7.1
  0+7+0, 7.1.4 4+7+0 with the upper layer at 30°, LFE nominally at 45°, −30°); **not BS.2051**: mono, LCR, quad
  (±45°, ±135°), 5.0, 7.0 (the 5.1/7.1 without LFE), **7.1.2** (top middle `U±090`) and **9.1.6** (7.1, wides
  `M±060`, then `U±045 U±090 U±135`) — `ituName` null, and `ear` cannot render 7.1.2 or 9.1.6 as speaker layouts.
- **Strips:** `ChannelStrip` gains `inputFormat`, `outputFormat` and `panner` (all required). **Decided:** the output
  format is **stored, kept equal to the destination's** by the server (the master's is the project's
  `settings.surroundFormat`; a bus's is the input format of the aux(es) on it, every aux on a bus having the same;
  `output:N` is stereo); it is never set directly. The master's input and output are the project's format. An input
  of the output's format passes straight through (beds). Sends to a multichannel bus are panned by the strip's panner
  (`Send.pan` applies to stereo buses only). `ProjectSettings.surroundFormat` (required) is the project's format.
- **Panners:** `PannerMode` (`stereo | surround | object`); `SurroundPanner(angle, diversity, elevation,
  centreLevelDb, lfeLevelDb, spread)` — **levels in dB** (−144…0) rather than the brief's bare "CentreLevel,
  LfeLevel", `spread` in degrees (0…180, a stereo input's two channels around the puck), `diversity` 0…1 (0 every
  speaker, 1 a point); `Default` (0, 1, 0, 0, −144, 60): front centre as a point, no LFE. `ObjectPanner(azimuth,
  elevation, distance, size)` (distance and size 0…1; `Default` (0, 0, 1, 0)). `Panner(mode, surround, object)` —
  **both panners' settings are always present** (they survive mode switches); `Panner.Default` (stereo).
  Automation and Smart Control addresses (`PannerTargets`, documented on `AutomationLane`): `surround:angle`,
  `surround:diversity`, `surround:elevation`, `surround:centre` (dB), `surround:lfe` (dB), `surround:spread`,
  `object:azimuth`, `object:elevation`, `object:distance`, `object:size` (`centre` and `spread` added to the brief's).
  Object mode requires the master as output in a surround project; at most 118 objects.

  the capacity behind skips to `WriteFrame − capacity/2` and marks a discontinuity. `SegmentHeader.CurrentFormat` is
  5: the playback segment's layout is format 4's, bumped so a host and server of different phases refuse each other's
  segments.

### Server domain decisions (workstream B)

- **Formats follow routing** (`Edits.Conform`, run by `Edits.Step` after any operation that upserts or removes a track,
  or changes the master or the settings, so job edits conform too): the master is in and out in the project's format;
  a track's output format is its destination's (the master's; the input format of the aux on its bus, stereo for a bus
  without an aux; stereo for `output:N`). The **panner mode follows the output**: the stereo pan below surround, the
  Surround Panner above it unless the strip is an object into the master (so `setSurroundFormat` to 7.1.4 puts every
  strip into the master on the Surround Panner, and back to stereo puts them on the stereo pan, objects included; the
  settings of both panners stay). `setPanner` refuses a mode its output does not allow. Packages conform on load.
- **Schema 10**: `settings.surroundFormat`, strips' formats and panners, markers' kinds and the project's `movie`
  (`ProjectFile.movie`, left out when none; a movie that fails `setMovie`'s checks reads as none).
- **Panner automation** compiles to targets `surround`/`object` with the parameter as `ParameterId`; `surround:centre`
  and `surround:lfe` are gains (as volume). Panner lanes record in touch/latch/write from `setPanner` without a mode.
- **Monitoring** goes to the host on connect, on change and when the project's format changes; a down-mix no narrower
  than the master is sent as none. **The HRTF set**: `Anode:HrtfPath`, else the first `*.anhrtf` in `hrtf/` beside the
  Server (a release bundles it there), else the first in `engine/artifacts/hrtf/` (where D's conversion writes it in
  development).
- **Movie**: copied into `Movies/<name>[ n].<ext>` (a free name) when `copyIntoProject`, else referenced by its
  absolute path; thumbnails are cached in `Movies/.thumbnails/<key>/<height>/<frame>.jpg`, the key a hash of the file's
  path, size and modification time. The stream is ASP.NET's range processing. Without FFmpeg the media jobs answer 503.
- **The tick ↔ movie mapping** is `Anode.Model.Time.MovieTime` (the editor mirrors it): movie time `m =
  seconds(p) − seconds(startTick) + offsetSeconds` through the tempo map **at nominal speed** (Varispeed scales both),
  frame `floor(m × rate)`; frame `f` plays from `seconds(startTick) + f / rate − offsetSeconds`. Scene cuts become
  markers at their frames' ticks (cuts before the project start are left out); Import Audio from Movie places the region
  at the first frame's tick, or at 0 with the file offset that plays there when the first frame is before the project
  start; Export Audio to Movie bounces the movie's span (Varispeed off), pads the start that is before the project start
  with silence, and down-mixes a surround bounce to stereo (D's `Downmixer`).
- **ADM export** is one stem render (the existing bounce with `tracks`) of every track to the master: an ordinary
  strip's stem is its post-fader signal as it reaches the master (in the master's format: a bed contribution), an
  object's stem its post-fader signal **before the object renderer** (its input channels, mono or stereo). The bed is
  the sum of the bed contributions, the objects their stems; both through the master's fader, not its inserts (the
  file is the renderer's input). The bed and object files given to D's writer are plain 32-bit float WAVs in layout
  order. **ADM import** brings each bed's and object's audio into `Audio Files` (up to 16 channels) and applies one
  `importTracks`, preceded by `setSurroundFormat` to the first bed's format when the project is stereo or narrower.
- **Listen-in**: one pump reads the tap every 10 ms and feeds each listener's framer; a socket's queue holds 1 s of
  frames and drops the oldest. `latencyMs` is what waited in the ring, plus a frame, plus a poll. The no-token origin
  check and the `access_token` query cover `/api/v1/remote/listen` as they do `/ws`.
- **Discovery** is advertised once the Server listens (its first address's port), again when the project's name
  changes, not when `Anode:Discovery` is false; the instance name is the machine's, the host its name with `.local`
  for an address on every interface. **Pads**: command ids are checked for form only (the editor knows the commands).

## Workstreams

Five, in parallel, each on its own branch; they meet only at the contracts above.

### A — Engine (`Anode.Ipc`, `Anode.Engine`, `Anode.EngineHost`, `Anode.Host`)

- **Multichannel graph:** strips, buses and the master carry their channel format; summing, sends, PDC and metering
  per channel; existing stereo paths unchanged in cost.
- **Panners in the strip** through workstream D's panners; objects collected at the master and rendered by D's
  renderer; down-mix and binaural monitoring after the master (D's down-mixer and binaural renderer); the speaker map
  to device outputs.
- **Effects:** run linear built-in effects per channel, link dynamics, stereo-only effects on L/R; instruments stay
  stereo (panned by the strip).
- **Multichannel bounce** (interleaved extensible WAV, or a file per speaker).
- **Listen-in tap:** the stereo master into a shared-memory ring (segment format 5) the server reads.
- **Tests:** multichannel routing and sums; PDC across formats; a stereo project bit-identical to Phase 9; panner
  automation sample-accurate; object rendering in the graph; monitoring never in bounces; no allocations in the
  callback with 7.1.4 and 32 objects; the CPU of a 7.1.4 session with 32 objects and binaural monitoring, recorded.

### B — Server domain (`Anode.Model`, `Anode.Server`)

- **Model:** every new command with validation and inverses; package schema 10 with a migration from 9 (everything
  stereo, no movie); remove the `PendingPhase10` placeholders; format changes that keep strips' routing valid (a
  track's output format follows its bus).
- **Compiler:** formats and panners into the host's model.
- **Movie:** import (probe through E, copy or reference the file — decide and document), metadata, thumbnails (E
  renders, B caches), scene detection to markers, audio import and export jobs (E's runner), the range-request stream.
- **ADM BWF:** export and import jobs through D's writer and reader and the multichannel bounce.
- **Remote:** DNS-SD `_anode._tcp` advertisement (reuse Phase 8's Bonjour code), `/system/discovery`, key-command pad
  sets in user settings, the listen-in WebSocket (reads A's ring, E's Opus encoder and framing, at most 4 listeners).
- **Tests:** properties for the commands; migration; the compiler's formats and panners; jobs with doubles; the
  listen-in socket with a fake ring; API tests.

### C — Editor (`editor/`; built with `editor/DESIGN.md`)

- **`remote` route:** transport, mixer with banks, Smart Controls, Live Loops grid and scenes, key-command pads, touch
  keyboard, drum pads, Step Sequencer, track list; phone and tablet layouts; touch-first.
- **Listen-in client:** WebSocket, Opus decoding (WebCodecs `AudioDecoder` where available, else a WebAssembly Opus
  decoder with a permissive licence), an AudioWorklet player with a small jitter buffer, latency display.
- **Surround:** the project's format in Project Settings; the Surround Panner (puck, diversity, LFE, elevation) and
  the 3D Object Panner (a top and a side view) in `audio-ui`, in the channel strip in place of the pan knob when the

- **Fake server:** every new endpoint and command; a fake listen-in stream (a tone) for the remote.
- **Tests:** stores; panner math (puck ↔ angle and diversity); the movie time mapping and drift correction; the
  listen-in jitter buffer; stories with axe for the panners and remote controls; the golden-path spec as `test.fixme`.

### D — Spatial DSP and ADM (`Anode.Dsp/Spatial`, `Anode.Interchange/Adm`)

- **Layouts** as data (ADM labels, positions per ITU-R BS.2051 for the formats in Scope).
- **Panners:** the Surround Panner (as Logic's: angle, diversity, centre, LFE), VBAP with the layout's triangulation
  (and the EBU ADM Renderer's point-source panner as the reference behaviour), object size spread.
- **Down-mix** (ITU-R BS.775 and the standard extensions for height layouts).
- **Binaural:** SADIE II HRTFs (convert the SOFA files offline with a script into a compact Anode format; check and
  record the licence in ADR-0008), partitioned FFT convolution per speaker (or per object), interpolation between
  measured directions; real-time safe.
- **ADM BWF:** writer (BW64 with `chna`, `axml`, `ds64` for large files) and reader, from a bed-and-objects
  description with automation.
- **Verification:** the EBU ADM Renderer (`ear`, Python; check its licence) in opt-in tests: validates Anode's files
  and renders them for comparison.
- **Tests:** panner gains against hand-worked values and `ear`'s; energy preservation; down-mix coefficients; binaural
  ILD/ITD at known angles; ADM round trips; `ear` comparisons.

### E — Media (`Anode.Media`, `scripts/`)

- **FFmpeg:** an LGPL build of `ffmpeg` and `ffprobe` (no GPL or non-free parts; the version pinned), fetched by
  `scripts/fetch-ffmpeg.mjs` by checksum for development and bundled beside the server as separate executables
  (ADR-0013); a runner with timeouts, progress and cancellation.
- **Operations:** probe (streams, frame rate, timecode, duration); extract audio at the project's rate; thumbnails
  (a strip of frames at given times, cached as JPEG); scene-cut detection (FFmpeg's scene score, with a threshold);
  remux with a new audio track (video stream copied; AAC or PCM in MOV); generate the golden path's test movie.
- **Opus:** an encoder for listen-in (Concentus, the C# Opus port, if its licence fits; else `libopus` through a small
  native shim), 48 kHz stereo, 20 ms frames; the listen-in frame format (sequence, transport sample position, packet).
- **Tests:** each operation against the generated test movie (frames, beeps, cuts known); remux preserves video
  packets; Opus round trip (decode with the same library) within the codec's tolerance; cancellation.

### Integration decisions

Settled when the five workstreams met (the contract changes are marked **contract**):
- **Real classes everywhere.** The host runs A's graph with D's DSP and HRTF set, the Server B's domain with D's ADM
  writer and reader and E's FFmpeg runner, Opus encoder and framer; nothing catches `NotImplementedException` any more
  and `Anode.Interchange`'s `Pending` is gone. `Phase10TwoProcessTests` runs them in two processes: a 7.1.4 bounce
  (a Surround-Panner track in Ls and the LFE, an object moving from L to the top rear right), Channel EQ on a 7.1.4 bus,
  the monitoring bounce (BS.775 down-mix coefficients within 0.1 dB; binaural ILD and ITD), the ADM export read back by
  D's reader, and listen-in from the host's tap decoded with E's Opus decoder. The 7.1.4 session's allocation and
  callback-cost tests also run with D's DSP.
- **The HRTF set** travels with the Server: its build and publish copy `sadie2-d2-48k.anhrtf`, `LICENSE` and `NOTICE`
  into `hrtf/` beside it (the packaged app's `resources/engine/hrtf/`; `publish-engine.mjs` checks them). Without that
  copy the Server finds the committed set from its folder up (`HrtfSet.Find`), not `engine/artifacts/hrtf`.
- **Surround WAVs.** The host's renders of more than two channels are WAVE_FORMAT_EXTENSIBLE in mask order; the Server
  read them as if in layout order (7.x's rears and sides swapped in the ADM bed and the movie export's down-mix).
  `WavInfo.ChannelMask` and `BounceProcessor.ReadLayout` read them back into the layout's (ADM) order, and a surround
  bounce is written extensible with the layout's mask (it was a plain WAV in mask order). `WaveChannels` moved to
  `Anode.Dsp.Spatial`.
- **ADM export** (replaces B's "one stem render" decision): the bed is A's bed-only master render
  (`RenderMessage.BedOnly`: every bed contribution, aux returns included, through the master's inserts and fader,
  without the objects), the objects a stem render of the object tracks only, each before the renderer and through the
  master's fader; every source is cut or padded to the scene's frames. B's sum of a multichannel stem per track was
  equivalent without master inserts but wrote a 7.1.4 file per track. An object has the channels the engine rendered
  (its stem's, one or two): the engine's width for a track is its own (a mono audio track without inserts is one
  channel) while new strips store a stereo input format, so the strip's format made a mono object stereo, 6 dB louder
  in `ear`. (Open: the stored input format of a mono track does not say mono.) The export dialog offers 24-bit (the default,
  which `ear` 2.1.0 reads correctly) and 32-bit float; 16-bit, which the Server refuses, is gone.
- **Diversity** is 1 = a point, 0 = every speaker in D's panner, C's Surround Panner (the puck's distance from the
  centre) and the fake Server alike: no change.
- **Per-channel meters** (**contract**): `TrackMeter.Peaks` (`Realtime.cs`), every channel's peak in layout order for a
  meter wider than stereo, read by the Server from the host's channel meters; `models.ts`'s `peaks` is that field.
- **Remote** (**contract**): `POST /remote/pads/run` (`RunKeyCommandRequest(commandId)`, 204) broadcasts the Phase 8
  `keyCommand` event, so a pad whose command acts on the desktop's views runs in the desktop editor; the remote's pads
  no longer show them disabled. A new user's pad sets are `RemotePads.Default` (the fake Server's `DefaultRemotePads`,
  by `keys/key-commands.ts` ids, which a spec checks), not none. Discovery advertises the first address a remote can
  reach (the desktop app's Server also listens on a loopback port, which it advertised before).
- **Movie media**: `GET /project/movie/frames/{frame}` renders a frame not in the cache (at most two FFmpeg runs at
  once; 404 past the end); the stream is `video/mp4` for MOV and M4V too (Chromium refuses `video/quicktime`); `Access`
  takes `?access_token=` on the movie's stream and frames as on the sockets, since `mediaUrl` gives it to `<video>` and
  `<img>`. The stream ignores other query parameters (the editor's `?v=`).
- **Bounce** (**contract**): `BounceRequest.monitoring` bounces what the monitoring sends the device (A's
  `RenderMessage.Monitoring`, a mix only): the golden path's binaural ears and down-mix.
- **Panner modes and strips**: B's compiler sends the whole `EngineStrip` (formats and panner) on every
  `setStrip`/`setChannel`/`loadModel`; `setSurroundFormat` puts the strips into the master on the Surround Panner and
  the editor follows the `trackUpserted` deltas (the golden path checks the mode after the switch).
- **Network access** (desktop): turning it on or off restarts the Server, which closed the open project; the app now
  refuses while the project has unsaved edits and opens the saved project again on the new Server.
- **The desktop editor's CSP** allows the Server in `img-src` and `media-src` (the movie's frames and stream load from
  it; the movie window fell back to "cannot play h264" and the movie track had no thumbnails).
- **Listen-in over plain HTTP.** A remote on the LAN opens `http://<host>.local:<port>`, not a secure context: no
  AudioWorklet and no WebCodecs. The player falls back to a `ScriptProcessorNode` over the same jitter buffer (512
  frames), the decoder to the WebAssembly Opus build; on a secure context (`localhost`, HTTPS) the worklet and WebCodecs
  play. The golden path hears both.
- **Renders after quick edits**: a render waits for every stream to hold its fill with the device's callbacks parked;
  the stale fills of a few quick edits filled the streams' queues and nothing dropped them (the stopped callback does),
  so it timed out ("the disk did not keep up"). The render's thread now drops them as it waits.
- **The test movie** alternates dark and bright backgrounds: E's palette gave the golden path's third cut (green to
  magenta) a scene score of 0.24, under Detect Scene Cuts' 0.4.
- **Golden path**: `remote-video-surround-golden-path.spec.ts` is a test; its helpers are `scripts/media-check.mjs`
  and `scripts/ear-check.py`, and the e2e global setup fetches FFmpeg (by checksum), makes the movie and sets up `ear`.

  silent, and checks the LFE send on a 60 Hz component (a 1 kHz tone is 74 dB down after the 120 Hz low-pass).
